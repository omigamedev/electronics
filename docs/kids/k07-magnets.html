🧲 Magnet Magic & Spinning Motors

Invisible pulls, magic nails and things that spin!

🧲 Two ends: N and S

⚡A magnet has two ends. One is called North, N. The other is called South, S. Different ends pull together. The same ends push apart!

🔄 Flip the magnet. 🤏 Then push them together.

Make the two magnets stick together! (Then try pushing the same ends together, too.)
N and S pull together and click! N and N (or S and S) push each other away.

🤔 Push or pull?

You hold N next to N. What happens?
  1. 🤗they pull together
  2. 🙅they push apart
Same ends push apart. Different ends pull together. Remember Sparky's balloons? Electric charges do the same trick!

✨ The invisible field

⚡A magnet pulls with an invisible field all around it. We can't see it… unless we sprinkle tiny bits of iron! They line up and show us the field's secret pattern.

✨ Tap sprinkle and watch the iron bits line up.

Sprinkle iron and see the invisible field!
The lines loop from one end of the magnet to the other. The field is strongest near the ends, where the bits crowd together.

🔩 Electricity makes a magnet!

⚡Here's real magic. When we Sparkies zoom around and around a coil of wire, we make a magnet! Wrap the wire around an iron nail, and the nail becomes a magnet too. It's called an electromagnet.

🌀 Slide to add more turns of wire. ⚡ Switch it on!

Pick up 5 paper clips! 📎
More turns of wire make a stronger magnet. That's why big electromagnets have LOTS of turns.
You switch the electricity OFF. What do the paper clips do?
  1. 👇they fall down
  2. 🧲they stay stuck
No electricity, no magnet! Electromagnets can be switched on and off. Giant ones in scrapyards lift whole cars, then drop them.

🌀 Motors spin!

⚡A motor has magnets and coils of wire inside. When we flow through the coils, magnets push and pull them round and round. Spin!
⚡And here's a twist: if YOU turn the motor, it makes electricity! Then it's called a generator. Pick a mode and try both!

🔋 Motor: the battery spins the fan. ✋ Generator: spin the handle round and round to light the bulb!

✋ Be a generator: crank fast enough to light the bulb brightly!
Spinning magnets near coils of wire push Sparkies along. That's how a wind-up torch works, and how big power stations make electricity!

🚗 Motors everywhere

What does a motor do?
  1. 🌀makes things spin
  2. ❄️makes things cold
  3. 🎨paints pictures
Motors spin wheels, fans, drills, toy cars and robot arms.
You turn a motor by hand, really fast. What can it do?
  1. 💡make electricity
  2. 🍪bake cookies
  3. 🧊make ice
A spinning motor is a generator: it makes electricity. Wind turbines are giant generators turned by the wind!

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Make your own electromagnet!

You need: 🔩 a big iron nail, a long piece of thin plastic-coated wire, 🔋 one AA battery, 📎 paper clips, and sticky tape.

  1. Wrap the wire around the nail, round and round, 20 times or more. Leave both ends free.
  2. A grown-up strips the plastic off the wire ends.
  3. Hold the wire ends on the battery ends for a few seconds, and pick up paper clips!
  4. Let go. Do the clips fall? Now try with more turns.
⚠️ Careful

The wire and battery get warm, so only connect for a few seconds at a time. Strong little magnets must never go in mouths. Play with magnets only with a grown-up.

👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
Current in a coil makes a magnetic field proportional to the number of turns times the current (ampere-turns); the iron core concentrates it. A DC motor uses the force on current-carrying coils in a magnet's field; spinning it by hand reverses the process (Faraday's law of induction), so it becomes a generator. Two or more small neodymium magnets are a serious hazard if swallowed. The AA "electromagnet" is a near short circuit: keep connections brief.
