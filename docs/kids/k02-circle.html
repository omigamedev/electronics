Level 2

🔁 The Circle Road

Sparky needs a road that goes all the way around.

💡 Sparky wants to light a bulb

⚡To light a bulb, my friends and I must travel out of the battery, through the bulb, and back into the battery. The road must make a full circle. We call it a circuit!
🔋 ➡️ 💡 ➡️ 🔋
⚡Circuit sounds like circle. That's how you remember it!

🚧 Fix the road!

⚡Oh no! The road has holes in it. We can't get through! Can you fix it?

👆 Tap each ❓ hole to put in a piece of wire.

Fill every hole and light the bulb!
With no holes, the road is a full circle. Sparky and friends can go all the way around, and the bulb lights up!

🔍 Spot the circle

⚡Look carefully. Follow the road with your finger, from one end of the battery to the other end.
Which bulb will light up?
  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
The road must leave one end of the battery and come back to the OTHER end, with no holes.
Only B is a full circle. A has a hole. In C, both wires go to the same end of the battery, so Sparky can't get back to the other end.

🌉 The switch is a drawbridge

⚡A switch is like a drawbridge. When it's open, there's a gap in the road. When it's closed, the road is joined up again. The light switch on your wall works just like this!

👆 Tap the switch to open and close it.

Turn the light on and off 3 times!
Closed switch: the road is a full circle, so the light is on. Open switch: there's a gap, so the light is off.

🤔 Open or closed?

The switch is open. What does the road look like?
  1. 🚧it has a gap
  2. 🛣️it's all joined up
An open switch makes a gap. No full circle means no light.
The switch is closed. What does the bulb do?
  1. 💡it lights up
  2. 🌑it stays dark
Closed switch means a full circle road, so the bulb shines.

🗺️ Sparky's trip

Put Sparky's trip in order. Tap the pieces one by one.
  1. 🔋 Leave the battery
  2. ➰ Zoom along the wire
  3. 💡 Go through the bulb
  4. 🔁 Back into the battery
Out of the battery, along the wire, through the bulb and back home. Then round again and again!

🚂 All at once!

⚡Here's something cool. The wire is already full of Sparkies, like a train with no gaps. When the switch closes, we ALL start moving at the same time, everywhere in the road!
⚡⚡⚡⚡⚡⚡
You flip the switch. When does the light come on?
  1. ⚡right away!
  2. 🐢after an hour
The light comes on right away, because Sparkies everywhere in the wire start moving together. Each Sparky actually moves quite slowly, but the whole train starts at once.

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Build a real circle road!

You need: 🔋 two AA batteries in a battery box, 💡 a small torch bulb (2.5 volts or 3 volts) in a holder, 🥈 kitchen foil, and sticky tape.

  1. Fold foil into long, thin strips. These are your wires.
  2. Tape one strip from the plus end of the battery box to one side of the bulb holder.
  3. Tape another strip from the other side of the bulb holder back to the minus end.
  4. Does it light? Now make a gap in one strip. What happens?
  5. Make a switch: two paper fasteners in cardboard, and a paper clip you can swing to touch both.
⚠️ Careful

Never join plus and minus with only foil and no bulb. That's a shortcut! The foil gets hot and the battery runs out.

👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
Current only flows around a closed loop: charge can't pile up anywhere, which is Kirchhoff's current law. An open switch is effectively an infinite resistance. The light comes on "instantly" because the wire is already full of electrons and the push travels close to light speed, even though each electron drifts at well under a millimetre per second. See Chapter 1 (drift vs. signal speed) and Chapter 2: DC Circuit Analysis.
