🔋 Pushers & Squeezers

Batteries push. Squeezy roads slow things down. Let's go down the water slide!

💪 The battery is a pusher

⚡We Sparkies don't move all by ourselves. We need a push! A battery is a pusher. It gives us energy to zoom around the circle road.
🔋💪⚡
⚡Think of a water slide. The battery carries us up to the top. Then, wheee, we slide down through the circuit!

🛝 The battery water slide

⚡Stack batteries to make the slide taller. A taller slide means a bigger push, and we go faster!

👆 Tap ➕ and ➖ to add or take away batteries.

Build the tallest slide with 4 batteries!
Four batteries in a row push four times as hard as one. Each AA battery pushes with 1.5 volts, so four of them push with 6 volts.

🔢 The push number

⚡Every battery has a push number. It's called volts. A bigger number means a bigger push!
🔋 1.5   🟦 9
Which battery pushes harder?
  1. 🔋1.5 volts
  2. 🟦9 volts
9 is a bigger number than 1.5, so the 9 volt battery pushes harder.
One AA battery pushes with 1.5 volts. How many AA batteries 🔋 do you need in a row to push with 3 volts?
1.5 and 1.5 more makes…?
Two! 1.5 + 1.5 = 3. That's why lots of toys take 2 batteries.

🤏 Squeezers

⚡Some parts make the road narrow and squeezy. They are called resistors. A squeezy road makes it hard for us to get through, so fewer of us pass by each second.
🛣️ ➡️ 🤏 ➡️ 🐌
The road gets narrow and squeezy. What happens?
  1. 🏎️more Sparkies get through
  2. 🐌fewer Sparkies get through
A squeezy road (a resistor) lets fewer Sparkies through each second. So a bulb on that road gets dimmer.

🎛️ Bright or dim?

⚡Now you are the boss! Pick how many batteries push, and how much the road squeezes.

🔋 Pick the batteries. 🤏 Slide to squeeze the road.

Make the bulb SUPER bright! 🌟
Biggest push, and no squeeze at all. Lots of Sparkies rush through the bulb.
Now make it glow just a tiny bit, like a night light. 🌙
A small push and a lot of squeezing means only a few Sparkies get through. The bulb glows softly.

🔥 Squeezing makes heat

⚡When we squeeze through a narrow road, we rub and bump and make it warm. Some machines squeeze us on purpose to make heat!
Which one uses squeezy wires to make heat?
  1. 🍞a toaster
  2. 🧊an ice cube
  3. 🪁a kite
A toaster has thin wires that squeeze the electricity so much they glow red hot. Never touch them!

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Test the push and the squeeze!

You need: 🔋 one and two AA batteries in battery boxes, 💡 a small torch bulb (2.5 volts) in a holder, ✏️ a soft pencil, paper, and foil wires.

  1. Light the bulb with one battery. Then with two in a row. Which is brighter?
  2. Draw a thick, dark pencil line on paper. Pencil lead lets a little electricity through. It's a squeezer!
  3. Put the pencil line in your circle road. Touch the wire near the start, then far along the line. Does the bulb get dimmer? If it won't glow at all, use a shorter line, or try an LED.
⚠️ Careful

Use only small batteries. Batteries and bulbs can get warm, so let a grown-up help.

👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
Voltage is energy per unit charge (the height of the slide); resistance sets how much current that voltage drives, I = V/R. Series cells add their voltages. The power turned into heat or light is P = I²R, which is why the bulb dims as you add series resistance. The pencil works because graphite is a modest conductor whose resistance grows with the length of the line. See Chapter 1 (voltage as energy) and Chapter 2 (Ohm's law, series circuits, power).
