🛣️ One Road or Two?

Two bulbs, one battery. How should we join them?

🤔 Two bulbs, one battery

⚡I have two bulbs and one battery. There are two ways to build the road!
🔋 ➡️ 💡💡
⚡One way: put the bulbs in a line, one after the other, like train carriages. Then every Sparky goes through both bulbs. The other way: give each bulb its own road, side by side.

🚂 Line road or 🛣️ side-by-side?

👆 Pick a road. Then tap a bulb to take it out (tap again to put it back).

🚂 In the LINE road, take out one bulb. What happens to the other one?
Both go dark! In a line road, the gap from the missing bulb breaks the whole circle.
🛣️ In the SIDE-BY-SIDE road, take out one bulb. What happens now?
The other bulb keeps shining! It still has its own full circle road.

🔍 What did you see?

Two bulbs in a LINE road. Are they brighter or dimmer than one bulb alone?
  1. 🕯️dimmer
  2. ☀️brighter
Dimmer. The two bulbs have to share the battery's push, and the road is twice as squeezy.
In a LINE road, one bulb breaks. What does the other bulb do?
  1. 🌑it goes dark too
  2. 💡it stays on
It goes dark. There's only one road, and now it has a gap.

🎄 The Christmas light mystery

⚡Oh no! Grandpa's old Christmas lights won't shine! They are all in one long line road. If just one bulb is broken, the whole string goes dark. Can you find the broken bulb?

👆 Tap a bulb to swap it for a new one.

Find the broken bulb and fix the lights!
You found it! With the broken bulb swapped, the line road is a full circle again. Newer light strings are built so one broken bulb doesn't spoil the rest.

🏠 Roads in your house

⚡In your house, every lamp and machine has its own side-by-side road. That's why the fridge keeps humming when you switch off your bedroom light!
How are the lights in your house joined?
  1. 🚂in one line road
  2. 🛣️side-by-side roads
Side by side! Each light gets the full push, and you can switch each one on and off by itself.

🗂️ Road facts

Which road is it? Tap a card, then tap a box.
🚂 Line road
🛣️ Side by side
  • 🌑 One goes out, all go out
  • 🔅 The bulbs share the push
  • 💡 Each bulb is bright
  • 🏠 Like the lights in a house
Line road (grown-ups say "series"): one path, shared push. Side by side (grown-ups say "parallel"): each bulb has its own path and the full push.

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Build both roads!

You need: 🔋 two AA batteries in a battery box, 💡💡 two small torch bulbs in holders (the same kind), and 🥈 foil strips or wires with clips.

  1. Light one bulb. Remember how bright it is.
  2. Now join two bulbs in a line. Are they dimmer?
  3. Unscrew one bulb. What happens to the other?
  4. Now give each bulb its own road, side by side. Unscrew one again!
⚠️ Careful

Bulbs get hot. Let them cool down before you unscrew them.

👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
In series the same current flows through both lamps and the supply voltage divides between them (Kirchhoff's voltage law), so with two equal lamps each gets half the voltage and, roughly, a quarter of the power. In parallel each lamp sees the full voltage, and the battery supplies the sum of the branch currents (so it runs down faster). Mains wiring in a house is parallel. See Chapter 2: DC Circuit Analysis.
