🥄 Who Lets Sparky Through?

Some things are open roads. Some things are walls!

🚪 Open roads and walls

⚡Some things let me zoom right through. They are called conductors. Metal things, like spoons and keys, are great conductors!
🥄 🔑 📎 ✅
⚡Other things are like walls. They block me! They are called insulators. Plastic, wood, rubber and cloth are insulators.
🧸 🪵 🧦 🛑

🧪 The Sparky tester

⚡Let's be scientists! Put a thing in the gap. If the bulb lights up, it lets me through.

👆 Tap a thing, or drag it into the gap.

Test 5 different things!
The metal things (spoon, paper clip, key, ring) light the bulb. Teddy, wood, crayon, sock and plastic cup block Sparky.

🗂️ Sort them out

Is it an open road or a wall? Tap a card, then tap a box.
✅ Lets Sparky through
🛑 Blocks Sparky
  • 🪙 coin
  • 🍴 metal fork
  • 🥈 kitchen foil
  • 🎈 balloon
  • 📕 book
  • 🥢 wooden chopsticks
Is it made of metal? Metal lets Sparky through.
Coins, forks and foil are metal: conductors. Balloons (rubber), books (paper) and chopsticks (wood) are insulators.

🔩 The secret

What is the same about all the things that let Sparky through?
  1. 🔩they are metal
  2. 🎨they are red
  3. 📏they are long
Metals have lots of Sparkies that are free to move. That's why metals are great conductors!

🧥 Wires wear coats

⚡A wire has metal inside, so I can zoom along. But it wears a plastic coat on the outside! Let's peek inside.

✂️ Tap the scissors to peel the coat.

Peel the wire and look inside!
Inside is shiny copper metal. That's Sparky's road. The plastic coat outside keeps Sparky in the wire.
Why does a wire wear a plastic coat?
  1. 🛡️to keep Sparky inside and us safe
  2. 🎀just to look pretty
  3. 🍬so it tastes nice
The coat is an insulator. It keeps the electricity inside the wire, so it can't jump into our fingers or into other wires.

💧 Water and you

⚡Tap water and salty water let some electricity through. And guess what? Your body is full of salty water! That's why water and wall electricity must never meet, and why we never touch sockets.
💧 + 🔌 = 🚫
Can you use a hair dryer in the bath?
  1. 👍yes, it's fine
  2. 🚫NO, never!
Never! Water lets electricity through, so plugged-in things must stay far away from baths, sinks and puddles.
An electrician fixes wires. Which handle is safest for her screwdriver?
  1. 🟥thick rubbery plastic
  2. 🔩bare metal
  3. 💦wet wood
Thick plastic and rubber are insulators, so they block electricity from reaching the electrician's hand. (She also switches the power off first!)

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Build your own Sparky tester!

You need: 🔋 two AA batteries in a battery box, 💡 a small torch bulb in a holder, and 🥈 three foil strips.

  1. Make a circle road with the battery and the bulb, but leave two foil ends free. That's the gap.
  2. Touch both free ends to a spoon. Does the bulb light?
  3. Now try a coin, a crayon, a leaf, a sock, a pencil's lead, and a plastic toy.
  4. Make two piles: things that light the bulb, and things that don't.
⚠️ Careful

Only test things with your battery tester. Never with a wall socket!

👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
In metals the outer electrons are not tied to any one atom, so a tiny field moves a huge number of them; in insulators the electrons are bound and a band gap blocks conduction. Tap water conducts because of dissolved ions, and skin is a modest resistor, which is why mains voltage can drive a dangerous current. Pencil graphite is in between (it may glow the bulb dimly or not at all). See Chapter 1, and Chapter 4 for semiconductors, the materials in between.
