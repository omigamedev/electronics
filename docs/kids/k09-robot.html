Level 9

🤖 The Robot Brain

Robots follow instructions. Let's be robot programmers!

🧠 A tiny computer brain

⚡Every robot has a brain. It's a tiny computer chip called a microcontroller. It's smaller than your fingernail!
🤖 🧠 📜
⚡The brain follows a list of instructions called a program. It does them one at a time, in order. Super fast, and it never gets bored!

👀 Senses and 💪 muscles

⚡A robot needs senses to find out about the world. These are called sensors. And it needs parts that DO things, like motors for muscles.
Is it a sense or a doer? Tap a card, then tap a box.
👀 Senses things
💪 Does things
  • 🌞 light sensor (eyes)
  • 🎤 microphone (ears)
  • 🔘 touch button
  • ⚙️ motor (muscles)
  • 🔊 speaker (voice)
  • 💡 light
Sensors bring information INTO the brain. Motors, speakers and lights let the brain DO things in the world.

📜 Order matters

⚡A program is like a recipe. If the steps are mixed up, things go silly! Help the robot brush its teeth.
Put the steps in order.
  1. 🪥 Pick up the toothbrush
  2. 🧴 Put on toothpaste
  3. 😁 Brush your teeth
  4. 💦 Rinse
Brush, then toothpaste? Silly! Robots do exactly what the program says, in order. So programmers think carefully about the order.
How does a robot brain follow its program?
  1. 1️⃣one step at a time, in order
  2. 🎲in any order it likes
  3. 😴it takes a nap
One step at a time, in order, exactly as written.

🕹️ Program the robot!

⚡Robo is hungry for energy! Make a program to drive Robo to the charger. Tap the arrows to add steps, then press go!

👆 Tap arrows to build the program. Tap a step in the program to remove it. ▶️ to run!

Puzzle 1: drive Robo to the charger. 🔋
Three steps to the right. Your first program!
Puzzle 2: go around the rocks! 🪨
Up, over, and down. Programmers often find a path around problems.
Puzzle 3: now Robo can only go forward or turn. Watch which way it faces! ↩️ ↪️
Real robots work like this: they drive forward and turn left or right. A turn doesn't move the robot, it just changes where it faces.

🐞 Bugs!

⚡Did Robo bump into something? That's called a bug: a mistake in the program. Every programmer makes bugs! The trick is to look carefully, find the bug, fix it and try again.
🐞 🔍 🔧 🎉

🌙 IF … THEN …

⚡Robots can make choices with IF and THEN. Robo has a light sensor for eyes. Here's his rule:
IF 🌙 it's dark THEN 💡 light ON

🌞 Slide to make it day or night.

Make it night, and see what Robo does!
The sensor sees that it's dark, so the rule says: light ON. That's how street lights and night lights know when to turn on!
Same rule. Now it's a sunny day. What is Robo's light?
  1. 💡ON
  2. ⚫OFF
It isn't dark, so the IF part is not true. Robo doesn't do the THEN part. The light stays off.

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Play the robot game! A grown-up is the robot.

You need: 🧸 a toy to be the "charger", and some space on the floor.

  1. Put the toy somewhere in the room.
  2. Write or draw arrows on paper for the program: forward, turn left, turn right.
  3. Read the program out loud. The robot must do EXACTLY what it says, one step at a time.
  4. Did the robot reach the toy? If not, find the bug and fix the program!
  5. Now swap. You be the robot!
👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
A microcontroller is a whole computer on one chip (CPU, memory and input/output pins) running one program in a loop. Sensors feed it inputs (often via an ADC or a digital bus); outputs drive LEDs directly and motors through driver transistors. The forward/turn puzzle is "turtle" control, close to how a differential-drive robot moves; the IF-THEN night light is a threshold on an ADC reading.
