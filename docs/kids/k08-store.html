Where does the energy wait until we need it?

🔋 A battery is an energy bucket

⚡A battery is like a bucket full of energy. Special stuff inside it gives us our push. When a toy runs, the bucket slowly empties. Some batteries can be filled up again with a charger!

▶️ Drive the car until the battery is empty. 🔌 Then charge it up!

Use up all the energy, then charge the battery full again!
A rechargeable battery can be emptied and filled again hundreds of times. Phones and tablets use rechargeable batteries.

📸 The quick bucket

⚡A capacitor is a different kind of bucket. It's small, but super quick! It fills up fast and empties in a flash. A camera flash uses one to make a big, bright, quick light.

⚡ Fill the quick bucket. 📸 Then take a photo!

Take 3 flash photos of the cat! 🐱
The capacitor fills up in a second, then dumps all its energy into the flash super fast. Flash!

🐢 or ⚡?

Which bucket fills up and empties super fast?
  1. ⚡the capacitor
  2. 🔋the battery
The capacitor is the quick bucket. The battery holds much more energy, but gives it out slowly, for hours.

⚠️ Sparky's battery rules

⚡Batteries are full of energy, so be careful! Keep them away from coins and keys in your pocket. Metal can make a shortcut, and the battery gets HOT. And if a battery looks puffy or leaky, don't touch it!
🔋 + 🔑🪙 = 🔥
You find a puffy, swollen battery. What do you do?
  1. 🗣️tell a grown-up
  2. 🔨squash it
  3. 👅lick it
Tell a grown-up and don't touch it. Puffy batteries can get very hot or catch fire.

♻️ Old batteries

⚡When a battery is used up, don't put it in the bin! Old batteries have useful metals inside, but also stuff that is bad for the Earth. They go to a special battery recycling box.

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Make a battery from lemons!

You need: 🍋 3 or 4 lemons, 🪙 copper coins or copper wire, 🔩 zinc-coated (galvanised) nails or screws, wires with clips, and a 🔴 red LED.

  1. Squeeze and roll each lemon to make it juicy.
  2. Push one coin and one nail into each lemon. Don't let them touch.
  3. Join the lemons in a line: the nail of one lemon to the coin of the next.
  4. Connect the LED to the two free ends: the long leg to the coin. Turn off the lights and look closely. A tiny glow!
⚠️ Careful

Don't eat the lemons afterwards. Wash your hands. Never cut open a real battery.

👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
A battery stores energy chemically and delivers it at a roughly steady voltage until it is depleted; a capacitor stores energy in an electric field (E = ½CV²) and can charge and discharge very quickly, which is why camera flashes, defibrillators and decoupling capacitors use them. Each lemon cell gives about 0.9 V (zinc and copper electrodes in citric acid) but very little current, so you need three or four in series for an LED. Coins and keys in a pocket can short a 9 V battery's terminals; swollen lithium cells are a fire risk. See Chapter 3 (capacitors and RC charging) and Chapter 5: Power for Robots (batteries).
