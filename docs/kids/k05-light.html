Level 5

💡 Light It Up!

Glowing bulbs, one-way doors and rainbow lights.

🔥 Bulbs get hot

⚡Inside an old-style bulb is a super thin wire. It squeezes us so much that it gets VERY hot, and glows! An LED is a newer kind of light. It makes light in a different way and stays much cooler.

👆 Switch them on and watch the thermometers 🌡️.

Which light gets hotter?
  1. 💡the old bulb
  2. 🔴the LED
The old bulb's thin wire gets hotter than an oven! That's why you must never touch a bulb that's on.

🚪 LEDs are one-way doors

⚡An LED is a one-way door for Sparkies. It only lights up if you put it in the right way round. Look at its legs! The long leg goes to the plus side of the battery. The short leg goes to the minus side.

🔄 Tap to flip the LED around.

Flip the LED so it lights up!
Long leg to plus, short leg to minus. Now the one-way door is open, and the LED glows!
Where does the LED's long leg go?
  1. ➕to the plus side
  2. ➖to the minus side
Long leg to plus. A handy trick: "plus" is the longer word for the longer leg!

🌈 Rainbow lights

⚡Some LEDs have three tiny lights inside: red, green and blue. Mix them to make new colours!

🔴 🟢 🔵 Slide to mix the light.

Make YELLOW light! 🟡
Try red and green together, with no blue.
Red light plus green light makes yellow light. Surprise! Light mixes differently from paint.
Now make WHITE light! ⚪
Red, green and blue all together make white. Your TV and tablet screens use tiny red, green and blue lights to make every colour!
Red light + green light = ?
  1. 🟡yellow
  2. 🟤brown
  3. 🔵blue
Yellow! Mixing coloured lights adds them together.

🪖 The LED's helmet

⚡LEDs are a bit delicate. If too many Sparkies rush in, poof, the LED breaks! So we give it a helmet: a resistor. It squeezes the road, so just the right number of us get through.

🪖 Put the helmet on or off. ⚡ Then switch on!

Light the LED safely!
With the resistor helmet, the LED gets just enough electricity to glow, and stays safe.

🌍 Saving energy

⚡Old bulbs turn most of their energy into heat. LEDs turn much more of it into light. So LEDs need less electricity to light up your room!
Which light needs less electricity?
  1. 💡old hot bulb
  2. 🔴cool LED
The LED! Less energy wasted as heat means less electricity needed.

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Make an LED glow with a coin battery!

You need: 🔴 an LED and 🪙 one coin cell battery (a CR2032).

  1. Find the LED's long leg and short leg.
  2. Slide the battery between the legs. The long leg touches the plus side (the side with writing on it).
  3. Does it glow? Now flip the battery over. What happens?
⚠️ Grown-ups only hold the coin battery

Coin batteries are very dangerous if swallowed. A grown-up keeps them, and puts them away right after.

👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
An LED is a diode: current flows only from anode (long leg) to cathode, and it needs about 1.8–3 V before it conducts. Above that, current rises very steeply, so you always limit it with a series resistor, R = (Vsupply − VF) / I (about 100 Ω for a red LED on two AA cells). The CR2032 trick works because the cell's own internal resistance limits the current. Keep coin cells away from children: a swallowed cell can cause severe internal burns within hours. Incandescent bulbs radiate mostly infrared (heat); LEDs convert far more of their power to visible light. See Chapter 4: Diodes, Transistors & Op-Amps and the LED circuit in Chapter 0.
