💡 Lights can talk!

⚡On, off, on, off. Blinks can carry a secret message! Long ago, sailors flashed lights from ship to ship using a code: short blinks and long blinks. It's called Morse code.
🚢 💡 · — ·
⚡The most famous message is SOS. It means: help! It's three short, three long, three short.

🔢 Counting with lights

⚡Computers talk with on and off too! They use lights or Sparky pulses to make numbers. Each bulb here has some dots. Count the dots of the bulbs that are on!

👆 Tap the bulbs to turn them on and off.

Make the number 5, like the fingers on one hand! 🖐️
Try the bulb with 4 dots and the bulb with 1 dot.
4 + 1 = 5. Computers store every number, picture and song as patterns of on and off. Grown-ups call it binary.

〰️ Wires carry messages

⚡Wires can carry messages too. When someone presses the doorbell, Sparkies zoom down the wire and ding-dong, the bell rings inside! Computers send super-fast on and off pulses down wires to talk to each other.
🔘 〰️〰️ 🔔

A wire! Pressing the button closes the circle road, and electricity rings the bell. (Some doorbells use radio instead, with no wire at all!)

📶 Invisible whispers

⚡Some messages need no wire at all! Bluetooth is like an invisible whisper. A phone sends radio waves through the air to your headphones. Radio waves are a cousin of light, but our eyes can't see them.

🔗 Tap to connect the headphones.

Connect the headphones and play the music! 🎵
Connected! The phone whispers the song to the headphones with radio waves, many times every second.

Invisible radio waves! Wi-Fi, walkie-talkies and the radio in a car use radio waves too.

🏠 Try it at home (with a grown-up)

⚡Send secret messages with a torch!

You need: 🔦 two torches, a dark room, and a paper with the Morse code for a few letters.

  1. Stand on opposite sides of the room.
  2. Blink SOS to each other: three short, three long, three short.
  3. Make up your own secret code! Maybe one blink means "yes" and two blinks mean "no".
  4. Blink the first letter of your name. Can your grown-up guess it?
👨‍👩‍👧 For grown-ups
Morse code is an early digital protocol: on/off symbols with defined timing (a dash is three dot-lengths). Serial links such as UART send bytes the same way, as timed high/low levels on a wire; the bulbs game is 4-bit binary. Bluetooth Low Energy uses 2.4 GHz radio (electromagnetic waves, like light but at a far lower frequency) and hops between channels. See Chapter 6: Digital Interfaces (UART, I²C, SPI) and Chapter 7 (ESP32 and BLE).
